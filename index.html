    After the donation

    Your journey continues
    after the chair.

    The centre's published guidance covers both the donor's recovery and the screening and processing of donated blood. These are two different parts of the safety journey.

    ♡FOR THE DONOR

    Recovery & aftercare

    Dr. Hedgewar's published process page includes the following general aftercare instructions. Follow the specific guidance given to you by the blood bank team.

    • 01
      Hydrate and eat

      The page advises drinking an extra two to three glasses of water and eating nutritious food.

    • 02
      Keep the bandage dry

      The published guidance says to keep the bandage on and dry for 10–12 hours.

    • 03
      Take it easy for the rest of the day

      The page advises avoiding heavy lifting and vigorous exercise for the rest of the day, and avoiding smoking or alcohol after donation.

    • 04
      Speak up about concerns

      Tell the staff promptly if you feel unwell during the visit. Contact the centre or a health professional if you have concerns after leaving.

    Source: Hedgewar Blood Donation Process ↗

    Screening & blood safety

    A donation is not simply collected and handed directly to a patient. The centre's donor consent information describes screening tests that are part of its blood-safety process.

    More than one product

    Blood components the centre lists

    The centre lists services involving these blood components. Not every whole-blood donation automatically produces each item; processing and clinical use depend on the blood centre's procedures and the patient's needs.

    A few helpful answers

    Questions are
    always welcome.

    It's normal to have questions, especially if this is your first time learning about donation. Here are a few starting points.

    Didn't find your answer? Tell us ↗
    ?!♡
    How long does the visit at Hedgewar usually take?

    The centre's published process page says the full visit—from arrival until leaving—usually takes around an hour, while the donation itself averages 5–10 minutes. Your actual visit can vary, so follow the team's guidance. Official process ↗

    What happens during registration and counselling?

    The centre's published description says staff sign donors in, go over basic eligibility criteria, share donation information and conduct a private, confidential interview about health history. Ask staff if any part of the process is unclear.

    What checks are described on the official process page?

    The published page lists checks of temperature, pulse, blood pressure and haemoglobin. These checks do not mean this website can decide eligibility; only authorised staff at the centre can make that assessment.

    Which screening tests are mentioned in donor consent?

    The centre's online donor consent page lists screening for HIV 1 and 2, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, syphilis and malaria. The page notes that screening tests are not the same as confirmatory diagnostic tests. Read donor consent ↗

    What happens after the donation is collected?

    The centre lists services involving packed red cells, platelet concentrate, fresh frozen plasma and single-donor platelet concentrate. The blood centre's own screening and processing procedures determine how each donation is handled.

    Can this website tell me whether I am eligible to donate?

    No. Eligibility needs an assessment by authorised blood bank staff using current guidance and your circumstances. Even if you read the centre's online eligibility page, confirm any questions directly with its medical team.

    Read the centre's published eligibility page ↗

    Where can I register or confirm the process?

    Use the centre's official website or contact it directly. The donor registration form requests personal details, so enter them only on the official website—not into this student CEP website.
